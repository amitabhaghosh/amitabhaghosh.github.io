Iridescent

Part of the broader vision of my research group (ANRG) at USC is to help create a better educated society. We aim to mentor and extend the power of knowledge to a diverse set of students at K-12 schools, and also at teaching-oriented undergraduate institutions. We'd like to enable them discover and explore the rewarding world of engineering and research, and expand their horizons in terms of further education and career opportunities in science and technology.

During my stay at USC, I collaborated with a non-profit organization, Iridescent (now Technovation), that works on science and engineering education at schools in Los Angeles, Bay Area, and New York. Their philosophy is summarized in the following quote, which is deeply consonant with my view as well:

“If you want to build a ship, don't drum up the people to gather wood, divide the work and give orders. Instead, teach them to yearn for the vast and endless sea.”

— Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

As part of the “Engineers as Teachers” fellowship program, I had volunteered for two semesters in the Garfield High School in Los Angeles, teaching a basic course on Electromagnetism and Wireless Sensors. My students had learning disabilities and came from the under-privileged society. Following are some of the course materials I had designed and used in class.
