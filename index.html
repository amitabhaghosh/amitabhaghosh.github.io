My work lies in the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI), computer networks, and distributed & embedded systems — spanning from design and analysis of algorithms and protocols, to modeling, optimization, reinforcement learning (RL), to building generative & agentic systems.

At Apple, I help design, architect, and develop generative and agentic models, and networking technologies for iOS, macOS, watchOS, and visionOS platforms. I'm privileged to work on products that make a difference in people's lives and help them realize their dreams. I thrive working with cross-functional engineering teams to create new partnerships, and help deliver elegant, scalable, and highly performant software & services in a dynamic, time-sensitive environment.

Previously, I ideated and led various SBIR/STTR projects with Department of Defense (DoD) agencies, winning grants, collaborating with business partners, and helping build prototypes and deploy advanced technologies.

In my academic life, I was a postdoc at Princeton and an Adjunct Faculty at USC. I've published over 25 research papers with 2500+ citations in top-tier IEEE/ACM journals and conferences, and served as a TPC member for many of them. I've also been invited as a speaker at many industry/university research labs, and as a panelist at the National Science Foundation (NSF) and the US Army Research Office (ARO).

Fun fact: My Erdős number is 3.

Bio

I got my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from the USC Viterbi School of Engineering in 2010, and was a postdoc at Princeton University from 2010 to 2012. At USC, I was part of the Autonomous Networks Research Group, and was advised by Prof. Bhaskar Krishnamachari. At Princeton, I worked in the EDGE Lab with Prof. Mung Chiang and Prof. Jennifer Rexford. In my dissertation, I worked on designing approximation algorithms for multi-channel scheduling, power control, and routing topologies to analyze throughput-delay tradeoffs for fast data collection in sensor networks (Thesis).

Prior to getting my Ph.D., I spent four years in the industry working on GSM/GPRS technologies at Lucent - Bell Labs, and on ad hoc networks at Honeywell. I got an M.E. in Computer Science and Engineering from Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, and a B.Sc. (Hons.) in Physics from Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur.

In the Spring of 2014, I had the opportunity to be a Guest Faculty at USC and teach a graduate course, EE 579: Wireless and Mobile Networks Design and Laboratory.
